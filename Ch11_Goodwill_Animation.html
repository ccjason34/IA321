Goodwill Is Recognized Only When Purchased
Goodwill represents future benefits from assets that cannot be individually identified and separately recognized. In class, the key is not “does the company have a good reputation?” The key is whether goodwill was acquired in a business combination.
📌 Best first question: did the company acquire another business? If not → no goodwill entry, ever.
Core rule: record goodwill only when one company buys another company and pays more than the fair value of identifiable net assets.
⚠
Student trap: internally generated goodwill is not recorded — even if the company has obvious goodwill in the form of brand strength, customer loyalty, or reputation. Only purchased goodwill goes on the books.
Purchased Goodwill
Recognized in a business combination when purchase price exceeds the fair value of identifiable net assets acquired.
Goodwill = Price - FV identifiable net assets
Internally Generated Goodwill
Created by reputation, loyal customers, strong employees, or excellent management. It may be valuable, but it is not recorded.
No journal entry
💰
Bargain purchase (negative goodwill). If purchase price < fair value of identifiable net assets, the buyer first reassesses fair values to ensure no measurement error. If the difference is still negative, the buyer recognizes a gain on the income statement immediately — not negative goodwill on the balance sheet.
Test cue: if the problem describes a business combination AND price > FV identifiable net assets → record goodwill. If price < FV identifiable net assets → bargain purchase gain. If no acquisition → no entry, period.

Goodwill Impairment
Goodwill is not amortized, but it can be written down if it becomes impaired. The basic classroom idea is: compare the reporting unit's carrying amount with its fair value.
Core impairment rule: if carrying amount is greater than fair value, record an impairment loss. The loss reduces goodwill. Student trap: do not record annual amortization first.
🏢
What's a "reporting unit"? An operating segment (or one level below). Goodwill impairment is tested at the reporting-unit level, not company-wide. This matters because goodwill from one acquisition might sit in a unit that's still healthy, even if the parent company overall is struggling.

🚫
No reversal under U.S. GAAP. Once goodwill is written down for impairment, the new lower carrying amount is permanent. Subsequent recoveries in fair value are not recognized — even if the reporting unit's fortunes turn around. (IFRS allows reversal for some intangibles but never for goodwill.)
Teaching point: goodwill impairment is a write-down, not amortization. Note: companies may first do an optional qualitative assessment; if that suggests no impairment is likely, they can skip the quantitative test. (Under ASU 2017-04, the test is now a single comparison of carrying amount vs fair value.)
Impairment of Other Intangibles
Other intangibles can also be impaired. The test depends on whether the intangible has a limited life or an indefinite life.
Core contrast: limited-life intangibles first use a recoverability test with undiscounted cash flows. Indefinite-life intangibles are not amortized, but are tested by comparing fair value with carrying amount. Student trap: do not use goodwill's reporting-unit test for every intangible.
