Commercial Substance Means the Economics Changed
This lesson covers only the standard commercial-substance case. When the exchange changes the company's expected cash flows in a meaningful way, the transaction is measured at fair value and the full gain or loss is recognized.
Core rule: If a noncash exchange has commercial substance, record the new asset at fair value and recognize the gain or loss on the OLD asset immediately.
⚠
Student trap: do not compare the new asset's fair value to the old asset's book value. The gain or loss is on the asset surrendered.
1. Remove the old asset
The old equipment and its accumulated depreciation leave the books.
Book value = Cost - Accum. Dep.
2. Recognize value given up
The fair value of the old asset measures the noncash consideration given.
Gain/Loss = FV old - BV old
3. Record the new asset
The new equipment is recorded at fair value.
New asset = FV old + cash paid

Why Debit or Credit?
Asset rule
Assets increase with debits and decrease with credits.
Contra-asset rule
Accumulated depreciation has a credit balance, so remove it with a debit.
Gain rule
Gains increase equity-like income, so they are credits.
Entry check
Every row either records what came in or removes what went out.
Click through the entry one row at a time. For each line, ask: did this account increase, decrease, or get removed?
Debit Equipment - new. Boc received a new asset. Equipment is an asset, and assets increase with debits. Asset coming in -> debit the asset
Debit Accumulated Depreciation. This sounds backward only because accumulated depreciation is a contra-asset with a normal credit balance. To remove a credit-balance account, debit it. Remove prior credit balance -> debit accumulated depreciation
Credit Equipment - old. The old equipment is leaving the books. Equipment is an asset, and assets decrease with credits. Asset going out -> credit the asset
Credit Cash. Boc paid cash as boot. Cash is an asset, and assets decrease with credits. Cash paid -> credit cash
Credit Gain on Exchange. The old equipment's fair value is higher than its book value, so Boc earned a gain. Gains are recorded with credits. $75,000 fair value - $60,000 book value = $15,000 gain
Check the entry: Debits are $140,000 and credits are $140,000. The gain is a credit because the old asset's fair value was greater than its book value.
