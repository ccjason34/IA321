Purchased vs Internally Created Intangibles

Kieso, Weygandt & Warfield · Intermediate Accounting 18th Ed. · Chapter 11 · Intangible Assets

How the Intangible Is Acquired Often Changes the Accounting
The same business idea can be capitalized when purchased but expensed when internally created. The reason is measurement reliability and specific accounting rules.
Core contrast: purchased intangibles usually have an objective purchase price, so they are often capitalized. Internally created intangibles often involve uncertain future benefits, so many related costs are expensed. Student trap: do not treat all internally created value as an asset.
Purchased From Outside Party
The company pays for a separable legal or contractual right. The purchase price gives objective evidence of cost.
Often capitalize at purchase cost
Internally Created
The company creates value through research, advertising, training, or operations. Accounting often requires expensing.
Often expense as incurred
Best first question: did Boc buy the intangible right from someone else, or did Boc create it internally?
