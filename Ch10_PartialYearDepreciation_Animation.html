Partial-Year Depreciation Uses Only the Time in Service
When an asset is purchased during the year, the company does not record a full year of depreciation. It records depreciation only for the portion of the year the asset was available for use.
📌 Memory cue: full method first, time slice second — but only for time-based methods.
Core rule: partial-year depreciation depends on which method you're using.
⏱ TIME-BASED methods (SL, DDB, SYD)
Full-year amount × time fraction
Compute the normal annual amount, then prorate (e.g. × 3/12).
📏 UNITS-OF-PRODUCTION
Rate × actual partial-year units
No separate fraction — actual units already reflect the partial year.
⚠
Student trap: the fraction starts when the asset is placed in service, not necessarily when cash is paid. And don't multiply UOP by 3/12 — that double-counts the partial year.
1. Find the Annual Amount
Use the normal method first: straight-line, declining-balance, SYD, or units-of-production.
Annual amount before proration
2. Find the Time Fraction
Count only the months the asset was in service during the year.
Months in service / 12
3. Prorate Depreciation
Multiply the annual amount by the partial-year fraction.
Annual depreciation x fraction
Memory cue: full method first, time slice second.

Apply the Fraction to Different Methods
Time-based methods use the 3/12 fraction. Units-of-production does not need a separate time fraction because actual activity already reflects the partial year.
Ready: choose a method and reveal the partial-year calculation.
⚠
SYD Year 2+ requires special handling. Subsequent years split between two SYD fractions because the SYD year boundary doesn't line up with the calendar year:
Year 2 = (Base × 6/21 × 9/12) + (Base × 5/21 × 3/12)
    = ($54,000 × 6/21 × 9/12) + ($54,000 × 5/21 × 3/12)
    = $11,571 + $3,214 = $14,786
The first 9 months still use the Year-1 SYD fraction (6/21); the last 3 months use the Year-2 fraction (5/21). This is a CPA-exam favorite.

Why Debit or Credit?
Expense rule
Expenses move like assets. Expenses increase with debits.
Contra-asset rule
Accumulated depreciation is a contra-asset. It increases with credits.
Partial-year rule
Time-based methods use 3/12; units-of-production uses actual miles.
Cash check
No cash is paid when depreciation is recorded.
