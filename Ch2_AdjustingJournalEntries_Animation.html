Adjusting Journal Entries

Kieso, Weygandt & Warfield · Intermediate Accounting 18th Ed. · Chapter 2 · Period-End Adjustments

Adjusting Entries Update Accounts Before Financial Statements
Adjusting entries are recorded at the end of the period so revenues are reported when earned and expenses are reported when incurred. They connect the unadjusted trial balance to the adjusted trial balance.
Why adjusting entries are required
Without adjusting entries, the financial statements would be incomplete or wrong.
Revenue may be missing Some revenue has been earned even though cash has not been collected.
Expenses may be missing Some costs have been incurred even though cash has not been paid.
Assets may be too high Prepaid assets and equipment may include amounts already used.
Liabilities may be wrong Amounts owed or revenue already earned must be updated.
Adjusting entries usually do not debit or credit Cash. Cash was received or paid earlier, or it will be received or paid later. The adjustment fixes the timing for revenue and expense.

Accrual Accounting Question
Ask two questions at the end of the period:

1. What revenue has been earned?
2. What expense has been incurred?

If the books do not yet show the correct amount, record an adjusting entry.
The Two-Sided Pattern
Every adjusting entry changes one income statement account and one balance sheet account.
Income statement side: Revenue or Expense
Balance sheet side: Asset or Liability
Memory hook: At period-end, adjust for what has been earned, used, owed, or receivable.
The Four Common Adjusting Entry Types
Most beginner adjusting entries fit one of these four patterns. The account names change, but the logic stays the same.
Prepaid Expense Used
The company paid before using the benefit. At period-end, move the used part from asset to expense.
Dr Expense
Cr Prepaid Asset
Unearned Revenue Earned
The customer paid before the company earned revenue. At period-end, move the earned part from liability to revenue.
Dr Unearned Revenue
Cr Revenue
Accrued Revenue
The company earned revenue but has not billed or collected cash yet. Record the receivable and revenue.
Dr Accounts Receivable
Cr Revenue
Accrued Expense
The company incurred an expense but has not paid cash yet. Record the expense and the liability.
Dr Expense
Cr Payable
Do not let the word "paid" trick you: adjusting entries focus on the part that belongs in this period. They are not normal cash receipt or cash payment entries.
