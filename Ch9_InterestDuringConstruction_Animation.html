The Setup: when can interest become part of the asset?
The project is a building under construction. Because it takes time to get ready for use, interest during the construction period can be capitalized.
Capitalization period starts when three things are true: expenditures are made, construction activities are in progress, and interest cost is being incurred. It ends when the asset is substantially complete and ready for use.

Step 1: weighted-average accumulated expenditures
WAAE weights each construction payment by the portion of the year that the company could incur interest on that payment.
Memory hook: amount paid times months outstanding equals weighted-average accumulated expenditures. A payment on December 31 gets 0 months in the current year.

Step 2: compute avoidable interest
Avoidable interest is the theoretical interest on the weighted-average accumulated expenditures. Use the specific construction borrowing first, then the weighted-average rate on other debt.
Why two rates? The specific construction note was taken out for this project, so its rate directly applies — but only up to its principal of $750,000. The remaining WAAE must come from somewhere too — the firm's other existing debt. Since money tied up in construction can't pay down that debt, we charge the leftover WAAE at the blended rate of those other borrowings.

Step 3: choose lower amount and record the journal entry
Actual interest is the ceiling. The company capitalizes the lower of actual interest and avoidable interest, then expenses the rest.
Final rule: capitalize the lower of avoidable interest or actual interest. In this example, avoidable interest is lower, so $120,228 becomes part of the building cost.
