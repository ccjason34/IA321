The First Question Is: Limited Life or Indefinite Life?
Students usually want to start with the journal entry. Slow them down one step: the useful life determines whether amortization is recorded.
📌 Best first question: can we identify a limited useful life? If yes → amortize. If no → no amortization (impairment review only).
Core rule: limited-life intangibles are amortized over their useful life. Indefinite-life intangibles are not amortized.
⚠
Student trap: "indefinite life" does not mean the asset lasts forever — it means there is no foreseeable limit on the period of benefit. Big difference on exams.
Limited-Life Intangible
The asset has a finite period of expected benefit, such as a patent with a limited useful life or a license that expires.
Amortize cost over useful life
Indefinite-Life Intangible
The asset is expected to provide benefits for an indefinite period, such as a renewable trademark with no foreseeable limit.
No amortization entry

Test cue: if the problem gives you a useful-life number, you're amortizing. If it says "indefinite," "perpetual," or "renewable indefinitely without significant cost," you're not.
Limited-Life Intangible: Calculate Amortization
For a limited-life intangible, amortization allocates the cost of the asset to expense over the periods that benefit from it. Straight-line is common unless another pattern better reflects use.
📐
Amortization period rule: use the lesser of useful life or legal life. Example: a patent's legal life is ~20 years, but if the company expects to use it for only 6 years, amortize over 6 years.

Teaching point: amortization is similar to depreciation in purpose, but it is used for intangible assets rather than tangible plant assets. Limited-life intangibles are also tested for impairment under the same two-step model as PP&E (see Ch 10). Partial-year acquisitions are prorated monthly, just like PP&E.
Indefinite-Life Intangibles: No Amortization
For indefinite-life intangibles, the key is what students should not do: do not record annual amortization expense just because time passed.

Important nuance: no amortization does not mean ignore the asset forever. Indefinite-life intangibles are reviewed for impairment when required.
