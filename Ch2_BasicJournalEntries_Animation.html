A Journal Entry Starts With Account Behavior
Students usually memorize debits and credits too early. A better first question is: what account changed, and did it increase or decrease?
Three-step habit: identify the accounts, decide whether each account increases or decreases, then apply the account's debit/credit rule. Trap: debit does not always mean increase, and credit does not always mean decrease.
Important pattern: Expenses and dividends move like assets. They increase with debits and decrease with credits. That is why many teachers group them as A + E + D: Assets, Expenses, Dividends.

Student shortcut: Assets, expenses, and dividends move together: debit to increase, credit to decrease. Liabilities, equity, and revenues move the opposite way: credit to increase, debit to decrease.
Analyze the Transaction Before Writing the Entry
Use the same questions every time: what happened, which accounts changed, did each account increase or decrease, and which side records that change?

Owner invests $10,000 cash for common stock.
Transaction 2
