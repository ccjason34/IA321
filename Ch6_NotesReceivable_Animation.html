Present Value Answers the First Measurement Question
When a long-term note receivable is accepted, the payee records the note at the present value of the cash expected to be collected. The note may equal face value, or it may carry a discount or premium when the stated rate and market rate differ.
Present value of note = PV of interest payments + PV of principal at maturity
1. Cash Interest
The stated rate computes the cash received each year. For a zero-interest note, this cash interest is zero.
Face value x stated rate
2. Present Value
The market rate discounts the promised cash flows. Interest payments are an ordinary annuity; principal is a single future sum.
Discount at market rate
3. Journal Entries
The JE follows the PV result. Face value goes into Notes Receivable, while the difference from PV is recorded as Discount or Premium.
Face value - PV = difference
Key habit: stated rate affects the cash interest line; market rate affects present value and interest revenue. Do not use the stated rate to discount the note unless the problem says it equals the market rate.

Related Journal Entries Follow the PV
The initial entry records the note at face value and uses Discount or Premium to bring the carrying amount to present value. Each year, interest revenue equals beginning carrying amount times the market rate.
