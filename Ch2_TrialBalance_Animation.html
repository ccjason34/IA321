The Trial Balance
After every journal entry is posted to the ledger, every account has a balance — either a net debit or a net credit. The trial balance collects all those balances into a single two-column list. If the accounting equation holds, total debits will equal total credits.
From the textbook: "A trial balance is a list of accounts and their balances at a given time. The primary purpose of a trial balance is to prove the mathematical equality of debits and credits after posting."

What it contains

Every ledger account, listed in chart-of-accounts order (Assets → Liabilities → Equity → Revenues → Expenses), with its ending balance placed in the Debit or Credit column.

When it's prepared

End of the period — before adjusting entries (unadjusted), after adjustments (adjusted), and after closing (post-closing).

Which column?

Normal balance side = the column used.
Debit column: Assets · Expenses · Dividends
Credit column: Liabilities · Equity · Revenues
Contra accounts go in the opposite column.

The equality rule

Total Debits must equal Total Credits — because every journal entry records equal debit and credit amounts. An imbalance signals a posting error.

Important limitation: A balanced trial balance does not mean the records are error-free. It only confirms mathematical equality. Errors that still hide include: posting to the wrong account, recording a transaction twice, or omitting a transaction entirely — as long as debits and credits stay equal, the trial balance still balances.
