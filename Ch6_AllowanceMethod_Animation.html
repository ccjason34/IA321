Receivables Are Reported at What You Expect to Collect
Selling on credit creates accounts receivable, but not every customer will pay. Chapter 6 treats bad debts as a normal risk of credit sales. The allowance method solves the timing problem: estimate the loss now, then use the allowance later when a specific account is written off.
Textbook anchor: GAAP focuses on reporting receivables at net realizable value: gross accounts receivable minus the estimated amount that will not be collected.

① Direct Write-Off
Records bad debt expense only when a specific customer is identified as uncollectible.
  • Simple, but usually too late.
  • Can mismatch expense with the related credit sale.
  • Normally not GAAP unless immaterial.
② Allowance Method
Estimates expected uncollectibles at the end of the period.
  • Debit Bad Debt Expense.
  • Credit Allowance for Doubtful Accounts.
  • Shows receivables at expected collection value.
③ Write-Off Later
When one account is later judged uncollectible, the write-off uses the allowance.
  • Debit Allowance.
  • Credit that customer's Accounts Receivable.
  • No new bad debt expense at write-off.
The mental model: Bad Debt Expense is the period's estimate. Allowance for Doubtful Accounts is the balance-sheet cushion that absorbs future write-offs.

Write-Off and Recovery: What Actually Moves?
A write-off is not the moment bad debt expense is recognized under the allowance method. Watch how the write-off reduces both Accounts Receivable and the Allowance, leaving net receivables unchanged.
