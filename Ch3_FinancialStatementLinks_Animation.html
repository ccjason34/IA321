Three Statements. Two Links.
The financial statements are not independent — they are connected by specific numbers that flow from one statement into the next. Two numbers do all the linking: Net Income and Retained Earnings.
Net Income — the first link
Retained Earnings — the second link
📈 Income Statement
Reports all revenues and expenses for the period. The bottom line — Net Income — is the first number that travels to the next statement.
Produces → Net Income: $4,050
Net Income $4,050 flows down
📋 Statement of Retained Earnings
Shows how Retained Earnings changed during the period. Takes Net Income from the Income Statement, subtracts dividends, and produces the Ending Retained Earnings balance.
Produces → Ending RE: $3,550
Ending RE $3,550 flows down
⚖️ Balance Sheet
Reports assets, liabilities, and equity at a point in time. The Ending Retained Earnings from the RE Statement plugs directly into the Stockholders' Equity section, completing the equation: Assets = Liabilities + Equity.
Why does this matter?
The linkages mean you cannot prepare statements in any order. You must prepare the Income Statement first (to get Net Income), then the Retained Earnings Statement (to get Ending RE), then the Balance Sheet last.

Link 1 — Net Income
Net Income ($4,050) is calculated on the Income Statement and immediately carried over to the Retained Earnings Statement as an addition to equity. It is the same number on both statements.
Link 2 — Retained Earnings
Ending Retained Earnings ($3,550) calculated on the RE Statement is carried directly into the Stockholders' Equity section of the Balance Sheet. It is the same number on both statements.
