The T-Account
Every account in the ledger can be drawn as a "T" shape. The account name sits at the top. Entries on the left side are always called debits (Dr.). Entries on the right side are always called credits (Cr.).
From the textbook (Illustration 2.2): "The terms debit and credit mean left and right, respectively. These terms do not mean increase or decrease." The positioning is simply an accounting convention — like driving on the right side of the road.

Key Rule: "Debit" and "Credit" are position labels, not value judgments.

Normal Balances by Account Type
Each account type has a "normal balance" — the side where increases are recorded. Click any card to see a detailed explanation and example entries.
From Illustration 2.2: "Increases to all asset and expense accounts occur on the left (debit side). Increases to all liability, revenue, and most stockholders' equity accounts occur on the right (credit side)."
🏦 Assets
Debit
+ Increase ✓
Credit
− Decrease
Normal Balance: DEBIT (Left)
Assets are things the company owns or controls (Cash, Equipment, Accounts Receivable, Supplies). They normally have a debit balance because we debit them to make them bigger.

Example — Cash:
Debit $10,000 (owner invests) → Cash increases
Credit $2,500 (pay for supplies) → Cash decreases
📋 Liabilities
Debit
− Decrease
Credit
+ Increase ✓
Normal Balance: CREDIT (Right)
Liabilities are amounts the company owes (Accounts Payable, Notes Payable, Wages Payable). They have a credit balance — we credit them to increase what is owed.

Example — Notes Payable:
Credit $5,000 (borrow from bank) → liability increases
Debit $5,000 (repay the note) → liability decreases
🏛️ Stockholders' Equity
Debit
− Decrease
Credit
+ Increase ✓
Normal Balance: CREDIT (Right)
Equity accounts (Common Stock, Retained Earnings) represent owners' claims. They have a credit normal balance — owners' interests grow on the credit side.

Example — Common Stock:
Credit $10,000 (issue shares) → equity increases
Debit (buyback shares) → equity decreases
💰 Revenues
Debit
− Decrease
Credit
+ Increase ✓
Normal Balance: CREDIT (Right)
Revenues (Service Revenue, Sales Revenue) increase equity by increasing profit. Since equity has a credit normal balance, so do revenues.

Example — Service Revenue:
Credit $8,000 (earned fees) → revenue increases
Debit (closing entry at year-end) → balance zeroed out
📉 Expenses
Debit
+ Increase ✓
Credit
− Decrease
Normal Balance: DEBIT (Left)
Expenses (Rent Expense, Wages Expense, Supplies Expense) reduce equity. Since they decrease equity (a credit-normal account), expenses carry a debit normal balance.

Example — Rent Expense:
Debit $1,200 (paid rent) → expense increases (equity decreases)
Credit (closing entry) → balance zeroed out at year-end
💸 Dividends
Debit
+ Increase ✓
Credit
− Decrease
Normal Balance: DEBIT (Left)
Dividends reduce retained earnings (equity). Like expenses, they decrease equity so they carry a debit normal balance — the opposite of equity accounts.

Example — Dividends:
Debit $500 (declare dividends) → dividends increase, equity decreases
Credit (closing entry) → balance zeroed out at year-end

The Expanded Accounting Equation
Normal balances follow directly from the accounting equation. Assets are on the left → debit normal balance. Liabilities and Equity are on the right → credit normal balance. Expenses and Dividends reduce equity, so they flip to debit.

Because assets sit on the left side of the equation, debits increase them. Because liabilities and equity sit on the right side, credits increase them. Expenses and dividends decrease equity, so they work in reverse — debit to increase.
