Two Ways to Tell the Same Story
Companies can present the same income data in two different formats. Both arrive at the same Net Income — but a multi-step format reveals more about where the profit came from along the way.
Key idea: Both formats use the exact same revenues and expenses and produce the same Net Income. They differ only in how the information is grouped and how many subtotals are shown.

① Single-Step Format

Two groups. One subtraction.

Structure:
  • All revenues grouped together → Total Revenues
  • All expenses grouped together → Total Expenses
  • Net Income = Total Revenues − Total Expenses
Strengths:
  • Simple to prepare and read
  • Avoids implying that some classes of revenue or expense are more important than others
Common users: Service companies, smaller firms, internal reporting.

② Multi-Step Format

Multiple subtotals. Operating vs. non-operating.

Structure (key subtotals):
  • Gross Profit = Net Sales − Cost of Goods Sold
  • Operating Income = Gross Profit − Operating Expenses
  • Income Before Tax = Operating Income ± Other Items
  • Net Income = Income Before Tax − Income Tax
Strengths:
  • Separates operating activities from non-operating items
  • Reveals profitability at each level (gross, operating, pre-tax, net)
  • More useful for analysis and decision-making
Common users: Merchandising and manufacturing companies, public companies, external reporting.
Why the multi-step is more informative: Imagine a company with growing revenue but falling Operating Income. The single-step format would hide this — you would just see Net Income going up or down. The multi-step format makes it obvious that the company's core operations are weakening even if non-operating gains keep total profit afloat.
Single-Step Income Statement
Everything reduces to one calculation: Total Revenues − Total Expenses = Net Income. All revenues are listed first, all expenses second. Below is a complete single-step Income Statement for Boc Apparel, a merchandising company.
Boc Apparel, Inc.

Total Expenses$249,000
Net Income$28,000
Notice what the single-step doesn't show: No Gross Profit. No Operating Income. No separation between operating and non-operating activities. Interest Revenue (a financing item) sits alongside Net Sales (an operating item). Interest Expense and Income Tax sit alongside operating costs. The structure is simple, but a lot of analytical detail is hidden.
