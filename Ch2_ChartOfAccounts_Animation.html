The Account Numbering System
Companies assign a number to every account. The first digit tells you immediately what type of account it is — like a postal code for the ledger. Pioneer Advertising uses a 3-digit system with ranges for each category.
From the textbook: "Companies arrange the ledger in the sequence in which they present the accounts in the financial statements, beginning with the balance sheet accounts. Each account is numbered for easier identification. Gaps are left to permit the insertion of new accounts as needed."

Why are there gaps in the numbering?
Notice that accounts jump from 101 → 112 → 113 → 126 → 130 → 157. These gaps are intentional. If Pioneer later needs a "Prepaid Rent" account, it can insert account 131 without renumbering everything. The same logic applies in every category — the chart of accounts is designed to grow.

How the Chart Links to Financial Statements
The account numbering mirrors the order in which accounts appear on the financial statements. Balance Sheet accounts come first (100s–300s), then Income Statement accounts (400s–900s).
Key insight: Every account belongs to exactly one financial statement. Knowing the account number tells you which statement it appears on and which side (debit or credit) is its normal balance.
